TITLE
-----
Knowledge-Enhanced Domain Adaptation of Large Language Models for Bilingual Railway Vocational Education under Resource Constraints

STRUCTURED ABSTRACT
-------------------
Purpose: The international expansion of railway technology has increased demand for bilingual vocational education, yet railway regulations, technical terminology and teaching materials remain difficult to access consistently across Chinese and English. Because most vocational institutions and local research teams work with limited computing resources, this study develops and evaluates a railway-domain large language model that answers bilingual railway questions on a single workstation.

Design/methodology/approach: The study addresses the problem with two methods. The first is a hybrid evidence-fusion method: BM25 lexical retrieval and BGE-M3 dense retrieval are fused by reciprocal rank fusion, and the top-ranked railway evidence passages are inserted into the prompt as the knowledge context for answering. The second is a vertical-domain adaptation method: completion-only QLoRA specialises Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and GLM-4-9B-Chat-HF on reviewed railway question-answer pairs, with the base weights frozen in 4-bit NF4. Both methods were evaluated on a held-out bilingual railway set against the original models and an unadapted Qwen3-14B reference under an identical single-GPU boundary.

Findings: Evidence fusion produced the highest retrieval quality in both languages, reaching Evidence Recall@5 of 0.715 in Chinese and 0.708 in English, and fusing both language fields gave the best cross-language balance. Vertical fine-tuning raised held-out character-level F1 from 0.189 to 0.398 in Chinese and from 0.437 to 0.648 in English for Qwen2.5-7B. Combining both methods gave the best answer quality: Qwen QLoRA reached an answer F1 of 0.660 in Chinese and 0.651 in English under fused evidence, and adding that evidence improved answer F1 for every evaluated generator in both languages.

Originality/value: The study shows that lexical-dense evidence fusion and parameter-efficient vertical adaptation are complementary rather than substitutable mechanisms for building a domain model, especially in specialised vertical domains. Reporting the two methods under one resource boundary and one held-out protocol separates the gain obtained from retrieved railway knowledge from the gain obtained from domain-specific fine-tuning, and it gives a reproducible procedure for developing vertical-domain language models without multi-GPU training.
